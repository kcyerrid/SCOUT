# APT34 (OilRig)

## 1. BLUF / Executive Summary
APT34 is widely treated as an alias for **OilRig (G0049)**, a **suspected Iran-linked** cyber espionage group active since at least **2014**. Public reporting characterizes the actor as **credential-focused** (phishing, account compromise, browser/credential-store harvesting) and as leveraging **web shells/IIS backdoors**, **PowerShell-based tooling**, and **compromised infrastructure** (including legitimate websites) to maintain access and support long-term collection. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

## 2. Attribution Notes
- **Primary anchor:** MITRE ATT&CK tracks OilRig as **G0049** and lists **APT34** among associated group names. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}
- **Iran nexus:** Open sources commonly describe OilRig/APT34 as *suspected Iranian*; however, public reporting varies in how explicitly it asserts direct state tasking. This note keeps sponsor fields conservative. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}
- **Naming collisions:** Vendor taxonomies include multiple labels (e.g., Crambus, Earth Simnavaz) that may refer to overlapping activity; treat these as tracking conventions rather than definitive separate entities. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

## 3. Motivations & Objectives
- **Motivation:** espionage / intelligence collection. :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
- **Objectives (reported):**
  - Obtain and reuse credentials to expand access and persist. :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
  - Maintain durable footholds in victim environments using server-side components (e.g., web shells) and scripted implants. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}
  - Collect sensitive documents and email-related data to support strategic intelligence requirements. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}

## 4. Targeting Profile
- **Regions:** primarily Middle East, with additional international targeting reported across multiple campaigns. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}
- **Sectors (commonly reported):** government, energy, telecom, finance, and technology/IT service providers (including enabling targets). :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}
- **Recent focus examples:** Israeli organizations in 2021–2022 campaigns (Outer Space / Juicy Mix) and a Middle East government intrusion described in 2023 reporting. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}

## 5. Tradecraft Overview
- **Initial access:** repeated **spearphishing** using attachments, links, and (in some reporting) social-platform delivery, with follow-on credential capture. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}
- **Account compromise:** compromise of email accounts to send convincing phishing, enabling expanded victim reach. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}
- **Credential harvesting:** tooling and scripts to extract credentials from browsers and Windows credential stores; credential-centric posture is repeatedly emphasized in major write-ups. :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}
- **C2/infrastructure:** use of DNS-based C2 in some tooling and compromised legitimate servers/websites as C2 or staging infrastructure. :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}
- **Persistence & lateral access:** use of Windows services and scheduled tasks, plus remote tooling and tunneling utilities where needed to maintain operational reach. :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}
- **Server-side access:** web shells and IIS backdoors are repeatedly described as a durable access method in OilRig tradecraft. :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}

## 9. Campaign History
- **2014-01-01 (at least):** MITRE ATT&CK reports OilRig activity since at least 2014. :contentReference[oaicite:30]{index=30}
- **2017-12-07:** Public reporting describes a targeted Middle East attack attributed to APT34, including use of a custom PowerShell backdoor following exploitation of an Office vulnerability. :contentReference[oaicite:31]{index=31}
- **2018 (multiple waves):** Unit 42 reporting documents OilRig campaigns and associated tooling such as [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/QUADAGENT]] and [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/OopsIE]]. :contentReference[oaicite:32]{index=32}
- **2021–2022:** ESET reports the Outer Space (2021) and Juicy Mix (2022) campaigns introducing first-stage backdoors [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Solar]] and [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Mango]] and use of compromised Israeli sites for C2. :contentReference[oaicite:33]{index=33}
- **2023-02 to 2023-09:** Symantec describes an eight-month intrusion against a Middle East government attributed to Crambus (aka OilRig/APT34), including an Exchange-focused PowerShell backdoor ([[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/PowerExchange]]). :contentReference[oaicite:34]{index=34}
- **2024-10-11:** Trend Micro reports Earth Simnavaz (APT34/OilRig) activity targeting entities in the Middle East, including Exchange-related tradecraft. :contentReference[oaicite:35]{index=35}

## 11. Defensive Recommendations
- Strengthen spearphishing resilience aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.001 - Phishing: Spearphishing Attachment]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.002 - Phishing: Spearphishing Link]] (high-risk user protections, attachment/link isolation, and rapid credential reset workflows).
- Prioritize detection for credential-store access aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1555 - Credentials from Password Stores]] and related sub-techniques (browser and Windows Credential Manager access patterns correlated with suspicious scripting).
- Improve visibility for script-heavy execution aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1059.001 - Command and Scripting Interpreter: PowerShell]] and scheduled/service persistence aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1053.005 - Scheduled Task/Job: Scheduled Task]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1543.003 - Create or Modify System Process: Windows Service]].
- Treat server-side artifacts and suspicious web-server behaviors as high-signal for [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1505.003 - Server Software Component: Web Shell]] investigations, especially on Exchange/IIS-adjacent infrastructure.
- Add network detections for anomalous DNS and tunneling behaviors aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1071.004 - Application Layer Protocol: DNS]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1572 - Protocol Tunneling]] when correlated with endpoint anomalies.

## 12. Analyst Notes
- “APT34” vs “OilRig” vs “Earth Simnavaz/Crambus” reflects tracking-label variation; use **G0049** as the stable normalization anchor. :contentReference[oaicite:36]{index=36}
- OilRig reporting frequently emphasizes **credential-driven operations** (harvesting and reuse) as a core enabling capability across multiple campaign eras. :contentReference[oaicite:37]{index=37}
- Public reporting spans multiple tool generations; avoid assuming a single static toolset when correlating detections (e.g., older IIS/backdoor tooling vs. newer first-stage backdoors reported in 2021–2022). :contentReference[oaicite:38]{index=38}
