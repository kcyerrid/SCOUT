# APT30

## 1. BLUF / Executive Summary
APT30 (MITRE ATT&CK **G0013**) is a suspected China-nexus cyber espionage actor publicly described as a decade-long operation focused on **Southeast Asia and India**, targeting government and select commercial entities for **political, economic, and military intelligence**. Reporting highlights unusually **stable tooling and infrastructure reuse** since at least 2005, including a set of backdoors and components designed to infect **removable drives** to support collection and potential **air-gapped network** data theft.

## 2. Attribution Notes
- MITRE ATT&CK describes APT30 as **suspected** to be associated with the Chinese government.
- MITRE further notes **Naikon** shares some characteristics with APT30 but does **not** appear to be an exact match, underscoring open-source cluster boundary uncertainty.
- Common alternate names in public tracking include **Override Panda** and Microsoft’s **RADIUM/Raspberry Typhoon**, among others.

## 3. Motivations & Objectives
- **Motivation:** Espionage and information theft aligned to strategic interests.
- **Objectives:** Long-term intelligence collection; durable access through stable backdoor ecosystems; document theft from connected hosts; and collection from **removable media** to facilitate air-gap bridging and staged exfiltration.

## 4. Targeting Profile
- **Primary focus (reported):** Southeast Asia and India, including ASEAN-associated organizations and regional political/security topics.
- **Sectors (reported):** Government and defense; select commercial entities with regional strategic value; journalists/media reporting on topics of interest to Chinese political legitimacy narratives.
- **Victim geography (examples reported across public summaries):** ASEAN member states and surrounding regional countries; additional suspected victim countries are enumerated in public tracking summaries.

## 5. Tradecraft Overview
- **Initial access:** Spearphishing with malicious document attachments consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.001 - Phishing: Spearphishing Attachment]] and user execution dependence consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1204.002 - User Execution: Malicious File]].
- **C2 architecture:** Reporting describes **two-stage C2** used to balance stealth and scalability, with long-lived domain usage across multiple years ([[Two-Stage C2]], [[Long-Lived Domains]]).
- **Air-gap and removable-media operations:** Dedicated components designed to infect removable drives to propagate and enable collection/exfiltration across disconnected environments consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1091 - Replication Through Removable Media]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1052.001 - Exfiltration Over Physical Medium: Exfiltration over USB]].
- **Host discovery & collection:** Backdoors associated with file/process and system discovery and registry interaction consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1083 - File and Directory Discovery]], [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1057 - Process Discovery]], [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1082 - System Information Discovery]], [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1012 - Query Registry]], and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1112 - Modify Registry]].
- **Staging & exfiltration:** Reported capabilities include local staging and archiving prior to exfiltration consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1074.001 - Data Staged: Local Data Staging]], [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1560.003 - Archive Collected Data: Archive via Custom Method]], and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1041 - Exfiltration Over C2 Channel]].

## 8. Infrastructure Patterns
- [[Two-Stage C2]] patterns to gate access to primary controllers and prioritize targets.
- [[Long-Lived Domains]] and infrastructure reuse across multi-year operations.
- [[Spearphishing Attachment]] delivery with regionally themed [[Decoy Documents]].
- [[Removable Media Propagation]] and [[Air-Gapped Network Bridging]] via USB infection and staged collection.

## 9. Campaign History
- **2004–2005 (reported):** Domain registration and malware compilation timelines described in public reporting indicate activity beginning in this window, with sustained operations “since at least 2005.”
- **2005–2014 (reported):** Reporting highlights repeated reuse of tooling and some domains over many years, including examples of domains remaining in use into late 2014.
- **2013 (reported):** Public reporting describes ASEAN-themed infrastructure and customized tooling appearing around ASEAN summit periods, consistent with the actor’s regional intelligence focus.
- **2015-04 (public disclosure):** A comprehensive public report details APT30’s tool ecosystem, workflows, and victimology across Southeast Asia and India.

## 11. Defensive Recommendations
- Emphasize resilience against attachment-led intrusions consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.001 - Phishing: Spearphishing Attachment]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1204.002 - User Execution: Malicious File]], especially for regionally relevant policy/defense and ASEAN-adjacent stakeholder communities.
- Apply strong controls and monitoring around removable media behaviors consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1091 - Replication Through Removable Media]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1052.001 - Exfiltration Over Physical Medium: Exfiltration over USB]] in environments where air-gapped bridging is a realistic risk.
- Hunt for persistence via startup artifacts consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1547.001 - Boot or Logon Autostart Execution: Registry Run Keys / Startup Folder]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1547.009 - Boot or Logon Autostart Execution: Shortcut Modification]] when correlated with suspicious staging and exfiltration patterns.
- Increase visibility into C2 behaviors consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1071.001 - Application Layer Protocol: Web Protocols]] and multi-stage/relay behaviors consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1104 - Multi-Stage Channels]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1090.001 - Proxy: Internal Proxy]].
- Treat concurrent registry discovery/modification and defense impairment behaviors (e.g., [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1012 - Query Registry]], [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1112 - Modify Registry]], [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1562.004 - Impair Defenses: Disable or Modify System Firewall]]) as higher-confidence signals when aligned with APT30 victimology.

## 12. Analyst Notes
- **Attribution confidence:** Medium (MITRE and major public reporting support a China-nexus suspicion; public evidence typically remains non-judicial and cluster-boundary ambiguity is explicitly noted).
- **Overlap caveat:** Naikon similarity is explicitly noted but not an exact match; avoid automatic merging of incidents without corroborating malware lineage and operational patterns.
- **Indicator discipline:** APT30 reporting emphasizes longevity and reuse; however, many concrete IOCs from older reporting can be stale. Behavioral + lineage correlation is recommended over IOC-only matching.
