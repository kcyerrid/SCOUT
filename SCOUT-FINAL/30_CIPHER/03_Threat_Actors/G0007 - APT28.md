# APT28

## 1. BLUF / Executive Summary
APT28 (MITRE ATT&CK **G0007**) is an attributed Russian state-sponsored cyber actor linked to Russia’s GRU Unit 26165 and assessed active since at least 2004. Public reporting consistently associates the group with long-running espionage activity and periodic influence operations (including hack-and-leak), with recurring targeting of government, defense, political organizations, and international bodies. MITRE ATT&CK documents a broad tooling set including [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Zebrocy]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/XTunnel]], and multiple platform-specific families (e.g., [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/XAgentOSX]]). 

## 2. Attribution Notes
- MITRE ATT&CK attributes APT28 to Russia’s General Staff Main Intelligence Directorate (GRU), specifically Unit 26165.  
- U.S. Department of Justice charging documents publicly connect GRU personnel and units (including Unit 26165 and Unit 74455) to cyber operations and related influence/disinformation activity; some cases describe coordination or support relationships with other GRU-linked elements (e.g., [[Sandworm Team]] in broader GRU contexts).  
- APT28 is tracked under many aliases across vendors and governments; use of [[Aliases]] alone is insufficient for analytic certainty without converging evidence.

## 3. Motivations & Objectives
- **Espionage:** Collection of sensitive diplomatic, military, political, and strategic information, often via durable access.  
- **Influence operations:** Selective theft and release of information to shape narratives and erode trust, aligned with hack-and-leak patterns referenced in public reporting and legal actions.  

## 4. Targeting Profile
- **Sectors:** Government, defense, military, political parties/campaigns, international organizations, NGOs/think tanks, and media are commonly reported targets; some advisories also describe activity against [[Logistics and transportation]] entities.  
- **Regions:** Strong emphasis on the United States and Europe, with sustained interest in Ukraine and NATO-aligned targets (as reflected across multi-year reporting and advisories).

## 5. Tradecraft Overview
- **Access acquisition:** Frequent reliance on targeted phishing lures and user-driven execution patterns consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.001 - Phishing: Spearphishing Attachment]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.002 - Phishing: Spearphishing Link]].  
- **Credential-centric operations:** Use of compromised credentials and credential replay behaviors consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1078 - Valid Accounts]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1550.002 - Use Alternate Authentication Material: Pass the Hash]].  
- **Infrastructure-heavy tradecraft:** Use of [[Lookalike Domains]] and [[Registered Domains]] for victim-facing lures and command-and-control, plus use of [[TOR]] and proxying patterns (documented in ATT&CK software associations such as [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Tor]]).  
- **Cloud and web-service enablement:** Use of web services for command-and-control (e.g., [[Google Drive]] as described in ATT&CK technique narratives) consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1102.002 - Web Service: Bidirectional Communication]].  
- **Specialized access vectors (reported):** Some public reporting documents non-traditional access and proximity-style approaches in a campaign context involving [[Wi-Fi Access Points]] (see MITRE campaign entry for APT28 Nearest Neighbor).

## 8. Infrastructure Patterns
- Use of [[Lookalike Domains]] / [[Registered Domains]] to mimic trusted organizations and deliver phishing and staging content (as described in MITRE technique narratives for APT28).  
- Use of [[Virtual Private Server]] infrastructure and relays, combined with multi-hop proxying behaviors consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1090.003 - Proxy: Multi-hop Proxy]].  
- Use of [[Web Service C2]] patterns, including third-party services such as [[Google Drive]], consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1102.002 - Web Service: Bidirectional Communication]].  
- Reported activity leveraging [[Compromised Routers]] and other [[SOHO Network Devices]] for operational access and relay in multiple government advisories.  

## 9. Campaign History
- **2004–present (public tracking):** MITRE ATT&CK assesses activity since at least 2004 and documents extensive tooling evolution across multiple platforms.  
- **2016 (high-profile influence operation):** Public reporting and investigations widely associate APT28 activity with compromise of U.S. political organizations and subsequent influence operations during the U.S. election cycle (as referenced in MITRE’s group description).  
- **2014–2018 (legal framing):** U.S. DOJ actions describe GRU cyber operations against multiple organizations (including anti-doping and international organizations) tied to GRU units; APT28 is cited in MITRE as connected to Unit 26165-linked personnel and operations in this period.  
- **2022–2024 (documented campaign):** MITRE ATT&CK’s [[APT28 Nearest Neighbor Campaign]] entry describes a campaign (Feb 2022–Nov 2024) involving proximity-style access via [[Wi-Fi Access Points]].

## 10. Known Indicators
No stable public indicators are included in this note. APT28 is a long-lived actor with frequent infrastructure refresh and reuse patterns; IOC handling should rely on incident-specific validation and current authoritative reporting.

## 11. Defensive Recommendations
- Emphasize resilience against targeted phishing and credential compromise aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.001 - Phishing: Spearphishing Attachment]], [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.002 - Phishing: Spearphishing Link]], and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1078 - Valid Accounts]].  
- Prioritize detections for credential replay and lateral access patterns consistent with [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1550.002 - Use Alternate Authentication Material: Pass the Hash]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1021.001 - Remote Services: Remote Desktop Protocol]].  
- Treat domain and web-service abuse signals (lookalike domains, suspicious web-service communications) as higher-confidence when correlated with victimology and known APT28 software families (e.g., [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Zebrocy]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/XTunnel]]).  
- Maintain awareness of advisory-reported tradecraft leveraging [[Compromised Routers]] and edge devices for relay and access, particularly where network-device telemetry is limited.

## 12. Analyst Notes
- **Attribution confidence:** High (strong convergence of MITRE attribution and government/legal actions describing GRU-linked operations).  
- **Alias management:** APT28’s alias set is unusually broad; avoid alias-only mapping and prefer convergent tradecraft plus software/infrastructure linkage.  
- **Technique scope:** ATT&CK technique associations represent observed reporting across time; not all techniques appear in every intrusion, and campaign-specific methods can vary substantially.
