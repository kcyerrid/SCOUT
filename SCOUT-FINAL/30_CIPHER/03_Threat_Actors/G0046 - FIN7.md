# FIN7

## 1. BLUF / Executive Summary
FIN7 (**G0046**) is a financially motivated cybercrime group assessed to have operated at scale against U.S. businesses since at least the mid-2010s, with major public law-enforcement actions and sentencing outcomes tied to large payment-card theft operations. MITRE and multiple vendors report that FIN7’s activity evolved over time, including a reported shift since ~2020 toward **big-game hunting** and ransomware/extortion-aligned operations (including reported use of [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/REvil]] and [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/DarkSide]]). FIN7 is also known for using a front company (“Combi Security”) as part of its operational model (reported).

## 2. Attribution Notes
- **MITRE ATT&CK** tracks the cluster as **FIN7 (G0046)** and notes long-running financially motivated operations and an evolution in monetization strategy (including ransomware-associated activity in later years).
- **U.S. DOJ (2018)** publicly attributed major intrusions and card-theft activity to FIN7 and described the group’s use of a front company (“Combi Security”) to recruit personnel.
- **FBI (2018)** published a victim-impact narrative aligned to the same indictments, emphasizing large-scale PoS and payment card theft impacts.
- **Vendor tracking** overlaps: CrowdStrike tracks the cluster as **Carbon Spider** and has published analysis focused on later “big game hunting” behavior; Mandiant has published research on FIN7’s evolution and clustering methodology.

## 3. Motivations & Objectives
- **Primary motivation:** financial gain through theft, fraud enablement, and (later) extortion-related monetization.
- **Objectives (reported):**
  - Access enterprise networks using social engineering and other initial access methods.
  - Reach payment processing/PoS environments (historical reporting) and/or high-value enterprise assets.
  - Enable theft/exfiltration and, in some cases, encryption for impact consistent with ransomware-enabled extortion patterns.

## 4. Targeting Profile
- **Industries (MITRE + DOJ/FBI reporting):** restaurant, hospitality, retail, gaming (notable in public indictments); plus broader U.S. industry targeting including software, consulting, financial services, medical equipment, cloud services, media, transportation, pharma, and utilities (MITRE).
- **Geography:** strong U.S. emphasis, with impacts abroad also described in public reporting (e.g., UK, Australia, France).

## 5. Tradecraft Overview
- **Initial access:** phishing campaigns using malicious attachments/links and document-based execution behaviors ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.001 - Phishing: Spearphishing Attachment]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.002 - Phishing: Spearphishing Link]]), including techniques leveraging Office execution features ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1559.002 - Inter-Process Communication: Dynamic Data Exchange]]).
- **Execution & post-compromise automation:** extensive scripting and command interpreter use ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1059.001 - Command and Scripting Interpreter: PowerShell]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1059.003 - Command and Scripting Interpreter: Windows Command Shell]]; related scripting sub-techniques).
- **Persistence:** common Windows persistence mechanisms such as registry autoruns, scheduled tasks, and services ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1547.001 - Boot or Logon Autostart Execution: Registry Run Keys / Startup Folder]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1053.005 - Scheduled Task/Job: Scheduled Task]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1543.003 - Create or Modify System Process: Windows Service]]).
- **Lateral movement & remote administration:** use of remote access tooling and remote services within environments ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1219 - Remote Access Tools]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1021.001 - Remote Services: Remote Desktop Protocol]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1021.005 - Remote Services: VNC]]).
- **C2 & data handling:** leveraging web services for C2 and cloud/web services for data transfer, plus staging and upload of payloads/capabilities (MITRE-reported) ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1102.002 - Web Service: Bidirectional Communication]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1567.002 - Exfiltration Over Web Service: Exfiltration to Cloud Storage]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1608.001 - Stage Capabilities: Upload Malware]]).
- **Operational evolution:** vendor and MITRE reporting describe later big-game hunting behaviors, including ransomware deployment and other impact activity ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1486 - Data Encrypted for Impact]]).

## 9. Campaign History
- **2013** — MITRE reports FIN7 active since at least this year.
- **2015–2018** — U.S. indictments and public reporting describe large-scale intrusions and payment card theft impacting hundreds of organizations, with downstream fraud/underground resale.
- **2018-08-01** — DOJ announces arrests/charges tied to FIN7 and details the “Combi Security” front company in public communications; FBI publishes a detailed overview aligned to indictments.
- **2020+** — MITRE and vendor reporting describe a shift toward **big-game hunting**, including ransomware/extortion-aligned operations.
- **2021** — Vendor reporting (e.g., CrowdStrike “Carbon Spider”) publishes analysis focused on big-game hunting evolution.
- **2022** — Mandiant publishes methodology and findings on FIN7 clustering and evolution across multiple activity sets.
- **2025-10-24** — MITRE ATT&CK page for FIN7 (G0046) updated (last modified date as posted by MITRE).

## 11. Defensive Recommendations
- Treat FIN7 as a **multi-stage enterprise intruder**: emphasize early detection of phishing-led access and post-compromise tooling patterns rather than relying on single-family malware signatures.
- Prioritize monitoring and response for behaviors aligned to **phishing + script execution** and **living-off-the-land abuse** ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1566.001 - Phishing: Spearphishing Attachment]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1059.001 - Command and Scripting Interpreter: PowerShell]]).
- Strengthen controls around **remote administration** and **remote access tooling** in enterprise environments ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1219 - Remote Access Tools]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1021.001 - Remote Services: Remote Desktop Protocol]]).
- Increase scrutiny for **web-service-based C2 and data movement** patterns consistent with FIN7 reporting ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1102.002 - Web Service: Bidirectional Communication]]; [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1567.002 - Exfiltration Over Web Service: Exfiltration to Cloud Storage]]).
- Include ransomware-focused readiness aligned to reported FIN7 evolution ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1486 - Data Encrypted for Impact]]), emphasizing rapid containment and recovery planning.
- Evaluate supply-chain and third-party exposure commensurate with reported tactics ([[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1195.002 - Supply Chain Compromise: Compromise Software Supply Chain]]).

## 12. Analyst Notes
- FIN7 has extensive aliasing across vendors; anchoring to **G0046** helps reduce naming drift (e.g., Carbon Spider, Sangria Tempest).
- Public reporting discusses overlap with “Carbanak Group” nomenclature; MITRE notes multiple groups have used [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Carbanak]], contributing to tracking ambiguity. This note treats FIN7 as a distinct cluster per MITRE.
- FIN7’s operational history is often described in phases (PoS/card theft → broader enterprise intrusion → BGH/ransomware); when scoping incidents, confirm which phase best matches observed behavior.
