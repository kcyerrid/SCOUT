# FIN6

## 1. BLUF / Executive Summary
FIN6 (MITRE ATT&CK **G0037**) is a financially motivated cybercrime group historically associated with large-scale payment card theft from **hospitality and retail PoS environments**, with reported evolution into **e-commerce (CNP) skimming** and **ransomware-enabled extortion**. Public reporting describes FIN6 leveraging a mix of phishing-driven initial access, commodity and dual-use tooling, credential theft, and data staging/exfiltration practices to monetize access at scale.

## 2. Attribution Notes
- FIN6 is tracked in MITRE ATT&CK as **G0037** with multiple associated names (e.g., **Skeleton Spider**, **ITG08**, **Magecart Group 6**). MITRE’s public entry focuses on observed tradecraft and victimology rather than definitive geographic attribution.
- Public sources attribute FIN6’s activity to organized cybercrime (financial motivation) and do not provide authoritative sponsor/state-tasking assertions.

## 3. Motivations & Objectives
- **Primary motivation:** financial gain through payment card monetization (sale/use of stolen card data).
- **Operational objectives:** compromise merchant environments; identify PoS and payment-adjacent systems; stage, exfiltrate, and monetize data.
- **Adaptive objectives (reported):** shift to **e-commerce skimming/CNP theft** when PoS deployment was constrained; leverage enterprise access for **ransomware deployment** in some intrusions.

## 4. Targeting Profile
- **Core sectors:** hospitality and retail merchants running PoS systems.
- **Expansion targets (reported):** e-commerce merchants and payment-server environments supporting checkout workflows; enterprises impacted by ransomware intrusions attributed/tied to FIN6 activity.
- **Geography:** global targeting footprint in public reporting.

## 5. Tradecraft Overview
- **Initial access:** phishing with malicious documents/attachments is repeatedly described; some reporting highlights recruiter-focused social engineering and cloud-hosted lure sites delivering malware payloads.
- **Execution & persistence:** common reliance on PowerShell, scheduled tasks, and service execution for staging and automation (aligned with MITRE technique reporting for FIN6).
- **Credential access & lateral movement:** credential dumping and valid-account use are emphasized in MITRE’s technique narratives; FIN6 also uses widely available tooling to facilitate movement and operations.
- **Collection & monetization path:** PoS-focused collection automation and e-commerce checkout compromise/web-skimming are both described in open reporting; exfiltration is commonly preceded by staging and archiving/encoding.

## 7. Malware & Tools Used
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/FrameworkPOS]] (aka [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/TRINITY]]) — PoS malware used by FIN6 for payment card theft (MITRE software entry).
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/More_eggs]] (associated: [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Terra Loader]]) — JScript backdoor used by FIN6 (MITRE software entry); reported as a preferred payload in recruiter/social-engineering campaigns.
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Stealer One]] — credential-stealer referenced in reporting on FIN6 operations against merchant environments.
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Cobalt Strike]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Metasploit]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Meterpreter]] — dual-use frameworks/tools repeatedly referenced in FIN6-linked intrusion narratives (MITRE group entry and public incident reporting).
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Mimikatz]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Windows Credential Editor]] — credential access tooling referenced in MITRE’s FIN6 technique descriptions.
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/AdFind]] — AD discovery utility listed in MITRE’s FIN6 software section.
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/PsExec]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Plink]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/PowerShell]] — commonly referenced operator tooling in FIN6’s ATT&CK-mapped behaviors (e.g., tunneling and script-based execution).
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/LockerGoga]] and [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Ryuk]] — ransomware families tied to FIN6 intrusions in public incident response reporting.

## 8. Infrastructure Patterns
- [[PoS system targeting]] with enterprise footholds to locate payment processing nodes and PoS endpoints.
- [[E-commerce checkout-page skimming]] / checkout injection patterns associated with “Magecart-style” monetization in public reporting.
- [[Cloud-hosted lure sites]] and [[Lookalike domains]] used to increase credibility of social engineering and reduce friction (reported in recruiter-focused campaigns).
- Use of [[Legitimate web services for payload hosting]] and [[Paste sites for staging]] to blend adversary activity with common outbound traffic patterns (MITRE technique narrative references web services).
- [[SSH tunneling for C2]] behaviors consistent with MITRE’s FIN6 technique reporting.

## 9. Campaign History
- **2015–2017 (reported):** FIN6 associated with aggressive PoS intrusions and large-scale payment card theft across hospitality/retail environments.
- **2017-09 (reported):** Visa reporting describes a shift toward **card-not-present (CNP)** theft via e-commerce compromise when PoS malware deployment was constrained, with e-commerce targeting amplified through 2018.
- **2018–2019 (reported):** Incident response reporting ties FIN6 tradecraft to ransomware deployment intrusions, with activity traced back to **2018-07** in at least one reporting stream, suggesting partial diversification from pure card-theft operations.
- **2025-06 (reported):** DomainTools describes recruiter-focused social engineering using trusted cloud infrastructure to deliver **More_eggs**, indicating continued evolution in initial access patterns and delivery infrastructure.

## 11. Defensive Recommendations
- Prioritize detection and control coverage for **merchant environments**: strong segmentation and monitoring around PoS networks and payment-server zones, with special attention to identity paths bridging user workstations to payment systems.
- Harden against **phishing and social engineering**: strengthen attachment/link protections and user execution controls, especially for business functions likely to receive inbound documents (e.g., HR/recruiting, vendor management, finance ops).
- Monitor for **dual-use tooling** abuse and unusual administrative activity: anomalous remote execution, scheduled task creation, service execution patterns, and suspicious credential access signals consistent with FIN6 ATT&CK mappings.
- Reduce web-skimming exposure: continuous integrity monitoring for checkout flows and third-party scripts, and rigorous change control for payment pages and e-commerce platforms.

## 12. Analyst Notes
- FIN6 has multiple overlapping ecosystem labels (e.g., ITG08, Magecart Group 6, Skeleton Spider). This note anchors to **MITRE G0037** to avoid alias drift.
- Public reporting supports an adaptive trajectory from PoS theft to e-commerce/CNP and, in some intrusions, ransomware-enabled extortion. Attribution of every ransomware intrusion to “FIN6 as a whole” can be complex; treat ransomware linkage as **reported ties** rather than universal FIN6 behavior in all cases.
