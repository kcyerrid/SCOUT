# FIN5

## 1. BLUF / Executive Summary
FIN5 (**G0053**) is a financially motivated cybercrime group reported active since at least **2008**, associated with theft of **payment card data** and **PII**, with a recurring focus on **hospitality (hotels), gaming/casinos, and restaurants**. Reporting highlights a tradecraft pattern centered on **abuse of legitimate remote access** (VPN/RDP/Citrix/VNC), internal discovery, credential dumping, and deployment of POS-focused malware such as [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/RawPOS]].

## 2. Attribution Notes
- MITRE ATT&CK tracks FIN5 as **G0053** and ties the cluster to a consistent set of behaviors and tooling over a multi-year period.  
- Public reporting notes the operator set likely includes **Russian-speaking** actors, but open sources do not provide a defensible country attribution; origin is left blank.  
- Many public descriptions emphasize “non-exploit” access paths (valid credentials and remote services) as a distinguishing operational hallmark.

## 3. Motivations & Objectives
- **Primary motivation:** financial gain via payment card theft and related monetization.  
- **Operational objectives (reported):**
  - Gain and retain remote access (often using stolen/abused legitimate credentials).
  - Traverse trusted relationships and internal segments to reach POS/payment environments.
  - Collect, stage, and extract cardholder data from memory-scraping workflows.
  - Reduce detection risk through artifact cleanup and event log manipulation.

## 4. Targeting Profile
- **Industries (reported):** hospitality/hotels, gaming/casinos, and restaurants/food service.  
- **Target characteristics:** environments with complex third-party administration, remote maintenance, and POS deployments—conditions that can increase credential exposure and lateral pathways.

## 5. Tradecraft Overview
- **Access & persistence:** heavy reliance on [[Legitimate remote access]] through remote services and valid credentials (VPN/RDP/Citrix/VNC) aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1133 - External Remote Services]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1078 - Valid Accounts]].  
- **Credential-centric operations:** credential acquisition and dumping to expand access, including use of [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Windows Credential Editor]] (mapped in MITRE to LSASS memory dumping).  
- **Discovery & mapping:** use of network discovery tools (e.g., [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/Essential NetTools]]) to identify targets and pathways aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1018 - Remote System Discovery]].  
- **Brute force support:** reporting describes password/credential scanning via [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/GET2 Penetrator]] aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1110 - Brute Force]].  
- **Tunneling / proxying:** use of [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/FLIPSIDE]] for RDP-based tunneling aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1572 - Protocol Tunneling]] and proxying behaviors aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1090.002 - Proxy: External Proxy]].  
- **Defense evasion / cleanup:** log clearing and secure deletion aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1070.001 - Indicator Removal: Clear Windows Event Logs]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1070.004 - Indicator Removal: File Deletion]]; secure deletion tooling is commonly referenced as [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/SDelete]].  
- **POS data theft:** deployment of [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/RawPOS]] and associated components ([[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/FIENDCRY]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/DUEBREW]], [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/DRIFTWOOD]]) to locate and encode cardholder data in victim environments.

## 8. Infrastructure Patterns
- [[Third-party/vendor access path]]: abuse of trusted remote administration channels used for POS maintenance (reported).  
- [[VPN access]] / [[RDP access]] / [[Citrix access]] / [[VNC access]] with valid credentials as recurring entry/retention pattern.  
- [[External proxy]] and [[RDP tunneling]] to maintain reachability into segmented environments.  
- [[POS network segment]] as the operational “destination” for theft workflows; staging may occur on adjacent non-POS systems (reported in related public write-ups).

## 9. Campaign History
- **2008-01-01 (at least)** — MITRE and reporting describe activity dating to at least 2008, including use of POS malware lineage associated with [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/RawPOS]].  
- **2015-10-13** — Public reporting describes FIN5’s reliance on legitimate credentials and highlights tools such as GET2 Penetrator and EssentialNet/Essential NetTools, plus credential dumping and cleanup behaviors.  
- **2017-02-16** — Public report publication detailing RawPOS family analysis and “intruder toolkit” themes connected to environments impacted by RawPOS-era activity.  
- **2025-04-25** — MITRE ATT&CK updates reflecting continued curation of FIN5 and associated software entries.

## 11. Defensive Recommendations
- Reduce credential abuse risk aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1078 - Valid Accounts]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1133 - External Remote Services]] by strengthening remote access governance, tightening third-party access oversight, and prioritizing monitoring for anomalous remote administration patterns.
- Increase detection for credential dumping activity aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1003.001 - OS Credential Dumping: LSASS Memory]] and suspicious use of credential-related tooling in administrative contexts.
- Harden and monitor POS-adjacent environments to reduce the blast radius of memory-scraper activity associated with [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/RawPOS]] (segmentation, least privilege, and focused telemetry around sensitive payment-processing hosts).
- Treat tunneling and proxy behaviors as high-signal when correlated with unusual admin activity (aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1572 - Protocol Tunneling]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1090.002 - Proxy: External Proxy]]).
- Improve log retention and tamper awareness to counter cleanup behaviors aligned to [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1070.001 - Indicator Removal: Clear Windows Event Logs]] and [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1070.004 - Indicator Removal: File Deletion]].

## 12. Analyst Notes
- FIN5 is frequently profiled as “credential-first” rather than “exploit-first,” making identity telemetry, remote access controls, and third-party access governance central to detection and prevention.
- FIN5 reporting spans many years; analysts should avoid assuming a single, static toolkit—commodity admin utilities and purpose-built components may vary by intrusion set and timeframe.
- Open reporting does not provide a defensible geographic attribution beyond “likely Russian-speaking”; treat origin inferences cautiously.
