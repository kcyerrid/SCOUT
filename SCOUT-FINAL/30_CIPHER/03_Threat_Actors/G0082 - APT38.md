## 1. BLUF / Executive Summary
APT38 (G0082) is documented in ATT&CK as a North Korean state-sponsored group specializing in financial cyber operations, with global targeting of banks, financial institutions, SWIFT endpoints, and cryptocurrency exchanges. ATT&CK reports a blend of common intrusion behaviors (PowerShell, service execution, discovery) and high-signal defense evasion/cleanup (EDR/AV disablement, firewall exceptioning, event log clearing, secure deletion), alongside malware families and destructive tooling associated with some operations.

## 2. Attribution Notes
ATT&CK attributes APT38 to North Korea and associates it with the Reconnaissance General Bureau in reporting cited on the ATT&CK page. This note treats attribution confidence as high insofar as it reflects ATT&CK’s stated attribution and sources.

## 3. Motivations & Objectives
- Financial theft and monetization from payment ecosystems
- Credential/host reconnaissance to enable lateral movement and operational execution
- Anti-forensics and cleanup to reduce investigation visibility
- In some cases, destructive actions (e.g., disk wipe / boot-record impacts) described in ATT&CK narratives

## 4. Targeting Profile
- **Primary victims:** Banks/financial institutions, casinos, crypto exchanges, SWIFT endpoints, ATMs (per ATT&CK)
- **Geography:** Global (multi-country targeting cited by ATT&CK)
- **Operational pattern:** Intrusions often include deliberate post-operation cleanup and control-plane hardening (firewall rules, log removal)

## 5. Tradecraft Overview
- **Initial execution:** User interaction pathways (malicious links/files) documented in ATT&CK.
- **Privilege & execution:** UAC bypass and service execution are reported as recurring patterns.
- **Discovery:** Browser info and filesystem discovery to profile users, internal resources, and operator pathways.
- **Defense evasion:** EDR/AV disablement, command history impairment, firewall exceptioning.
- **Cleanup:** Clearing event logs, secure deletion, timestomping to hinder timeline reconstruction.

## 8. Infrastructure Patterns
- Lookalike domains to impersonate legitimate institutions (Acquire Infrastructure: Domains).
- HTTP/HTTPS-style C2 channels and operator workflows.
- Firewall exemptions on selected ports as part of defense evasion and operational reliability (per ATT&CK technique narrative).

## 9. Campaign History
- **2014–present (reported):** Ongoing financial cyber operations; ATT&CK cites major incidents and sustained activity across many countries.

## 10. Known Indicators
No stable public IOCs are included. Use case telemetry (proxy/DNS, endpoint process lineage, Windows event logs, Sysmon) for incident-specific hunting.

## 11. Defensive Recommendations
- **High-signal controls:** Alert on EDR/AV “unhooking” and tamper events; treat them as near-critical.
- Detect creation/modification of Windows services with suspicious binary paths; correlate with new executable drops and immediate outbound beacons.
- Monitor for firewall rule changes and exemptions, especially on unusual admin/application ports; baseline expected change windows.
- Prioritize detections for event log clearing + secure deletion behavior sequences (log clear → file deletion → timestomp).
- Harden authentication: mitigate brute force/password spraying patterns and monitor anomalous logons tied to financial operator workstations and SWIFT endpoints.

## 12. Analyst Notes
**Confidence:** High for described behaviors, techniques, and listed ATT&CK software. Overlap among North Korean clusters is explicitly noted by ATT&CK; avoid assuming equivalence across cluster names unless explicitly stated in the cited sources.
