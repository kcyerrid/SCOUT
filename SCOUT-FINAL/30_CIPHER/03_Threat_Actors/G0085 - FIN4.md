## 1. BLUF / Executive Summary
FIN4 (G0085) is documented in ATT&CK as a financially motivated group targeting non-public market-moving information—especially within healthcare and pharmaceutical organizations—since at least 2013. ATT&CK highlights FIN4’s emphasis on **credential theft and email access** (including Outlook/OWA workflows), leveraging phishing, input capture, mailbox rule manipulation, and anonymized access patterns (multi-hop proxy/Tor) rather than classic persistent malware deployment.

## 2. Attribution Notes
ATT&CK describes FIN4 as financially motivated and does not assign state sponsorship. This note maintains that framing and avoids extending attribution beyond ATT&CK’s referenced reporting.

## 3. Motivations & Objectives
- Obtain credentials to email accounts and internal correspondence portals
- Hijack remote email access to monitor sensitive communications
- Reduce detection by suppressing victim awareness and internal warnings (mailbox hiding rules)

## 4. Targeting Profile
- **Primary focus (ATT&CK):** Healthcare and pharmaceutical companies; information impacting public financial markets
- **Key assets:** Mailboxes (OWA/Outlook), identity systems, and business communication platforms

## 5. Tradecraft Overview
- **Phishing:** Attachments with embedded macros and links, often delivered from compromised accounts.
- **Credential capture:** Fake OWA pages, spoofed Windows authentication prompts, and keylogging.
- **Email takeover:** Remote email collection using stolen credentials and hijacked sessions.
- **Stealth:** Inbox/Outlook rules to delete or hide warning messages; anonymized logins via multi-hop proxy/Tor.

## 7. Malware & Tools Used
- **Malware:** ATT&CK emphasizes credential capture and email access; no ATT&CK software entries are listed on the FIN4 page.
- **Tools / enabling mechanisms (ATT&CK narrative):**
  - [[Tor]] (used for access to victim email accounts, per ATT&CK)
  - [[VBA Macros]] / Visual Basic macro execution for credential prompt collection

## 8. Infrastructure Patterns
- Compromised email accounts used to deliver lures (“internal-looking” spearphish)
- Web-based credential capture targeting OWA and similar portals
- Access anonymization via Tor/multi-hop proxy workflows

## 9. Campaign History
- **2013–present (reported):** Credential-centric intrusions targeting market-sensitive correspondence in healthcare/pharma (per ATT&CK summary).

## 10. Known Indicators
No stable indicators included. This actor’s strength is workflow abuse rather than fixed malware infrastructure; rely on identity and mail telemetry.

## 11. Defensive Recommendations
- Enforce phishing-resistant MFA for email and remote access; restrict legacy auth; implement conditional access and geo/ASN anomaly controls.
- Monitor for anomalous mailbox rules: auto-delete/move rules containing security terms; sudden rule creation after suspicious sign-in.
- Detect suspicious OWA sign-ins via Tor/proxy ASNs and impossible travel; correlate with mailbox access patterns and OAuth/app consent anomalies (where applicable).
- Flag user-reported credential prompts and spoofed authentication dialogs; increase logging around browser credential submissions and SSO events.

## 12. Analyst Notes
**Confidence:** High on behaviors listed due to explicit ATT&CK technique narratives. FIN4-style operations often leave the strongest traces in **identity + email audit logs**, not endpoint malware artifacts.
