## 1. Summary
Evil Twin attacks use a rogue Wi-Fi access point configured to mimic a legitimate network (often via SSID cloning) to lure victims into connecting. Once connected, the adversary can collect credentials (e.g., via fake captive portals) and monitor or manipulate victim traffic.

## 2. Technical Overview
- Adversary deploys a malicious AP advertising the same (or deceptively similar) SSID as a target network, often with stronger signal or opportunistic placement.
- Victims connect to the fraudulent AP and may be presented with:
  - Fake login pages/captive portals to capture credentials
  - Downstream redirection or interception of network sessions
- After association, adversary can monitor network activity, manipulate data, or steal additional credentials.

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
- Evil Twin is distinct from wired LAN AiTM (ARP/DHCP) by its reliance on **wireless telemetry** and **WIPS controls**.
- Coverage should include:
  - Guest/public Wi-Fi environments (higher exposure)
  - Corporate SSIDs with roaming users
- Detection must account for legitimate AP changes (BSSID rotations, mesh networks) to reduce false positives.

## 4. Procedure Examples
- [[30_CIPHER/03_Threat_Actors/G0007 - APT28|APT28]]: used a Wi-Fi Pineapple to set up Evil Twin Wi-Fi poisoning to capture victim credentials and/or plant espionage-oriented malware.

## 5. Detection Guidance
MITRE detection strategy emphasizes identifying rogue APs broadcasting the same SSID:
- **Wireless indicators**
  - Duplicate SSID with unexpected BSSID/MAC, manufacturer OUI anomalies, or inconsistent encryption settings
  - Strong signal AP appearing suddenly in proximity to known locations
  - Clients roaming/associating to non-corporate BSSIDs for a protected SSID
- **User/auth indicators**
  - Captive portal redirects on networks that should not present portals
  - Repeated login prompts or credential submissions after association events
- **Traffic indicators**
  - Anomalous traffic paths through unauthorized APs; new DNS/gateway behavior after wireless association

### Data Source Notes
Telemetry commonly needed:
- WIPS/WLAN controller logs (rogue AP detection, association events, BSSID inventory)
- DHCP/DNS logs correlated with wireless associations
- Web proxy logs for captive portal redirects and suspicious login pages

## 6. Response Guidance
1. Confirm rogue AP presence via WIPS/WLAN controller and physical/security procedures.
2. Deauthenticate/contain rogue AP where possible and block client associations (controller/WIPS).
3. Identify exposed credentials and rotate passwords/tokens; prioritize accounts seen authenticating via captive portal flows.
4. Notify users and reinforce safe Wi-Fi practices; validate certificate/trust warnings were not ignored.

## 8. SOC Relevance
Evil Twin attacks are especially relevant for:
- Exec/travel scenarios (airports, cafes, conferences)
- Environments with frequent remote/hybrid work
- Incident response for suspected credential compromise without endpoint malware

High-value correlations:
- Rogue AP detection + captive portal redirects + credential submission spikes.

## 12. Mitigations
- **M1031 Network Intrusion Prevention:** Use wireless intrusion prevention systems (WIPS) to detect rogue APs and Evil Twin patterns.
- **M1017 User Training:** Train users to avoid open/unsecured networks and treat certificate errors as suspicious; emphasize verification of expected Wi-Fi networks.

## 13. Testing & Validation
- Validate WIPS rules detect:
  - Duplicate SSIDs with unexpected BSSIDs
  - Encryption mismatches for known SSIDs
  - Captive portal redirects on corporate SSIDs
- Conduct controlled wireless security assessments (authorized only) to ensure SOC procedures for rogue AP triage and credential response are effective.
