# Spearphishing via Service (T1566.003)

## 1. Summary
Spearphishing via Service describes adversary use of legitimate third-party services—such as email providers, collaboration platforms, social media, or cloud messaging services—to deliver phishing content. By abusing trusted platforms, adversaries increase message credibility and reduce the effectiveness of traditional email security controls.

This subtechnique is increasingly prevalent as organizations adopt cloud and SaaS collaboration tools.

---

## 2. Technical Overview
Adversaries leverage trusted services to send phishing messages that contain malicious links, requests for credentials, or instructions for follow-on actions.

Commonly abused services include:
- Cloud email providers
- Collaboration platforms (e.g., chat, file sharing)
- Social media and professional networking platforms
- Customer support or ticketing systems

Artifacts include application audit logs, message metadata, user click events, authentication attempts, and downstream endpoint or identity activity.

---

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
T1566.003 differs from other phishing subtechniques by:
- Using **legitimate service infrastructure** rather than direct email delivery
- Often bypassing email gateways entirely
- Leveraging platform trust, branding, and native notifications

Effectiveness depends on service logging depth and identity protection controls.

---

## 4. Procedure Examples
Observed adversary procedures include:
- Sending malicious links via collaboration platform direct messages
- Abusing compromised SaaS accounts to message internal users
- Impersonating support or workflow notifications
- Using cloud file-sharing links to stage payloads or credential harvesters

Analysts may observe legitimate service activity followed by suspicious authentication or endpoint behavior.

---

## 5. Detection Guidance
Detection should emphasize service and identity telemetry:
- Messages originating from newly created or compromised accounts
- Unusual messaging behavior (volume, recipients, timing)
- User clicks followed by abnormal authentication attempts
- Links to newly registered or low-reputation domains

Cross-platform correlation is critical due to multi-service abuse.

### Data Source Notes
- **Application Logs:** Primary source for service-based phishing detection
- **Authentication Logs:** Identify credential misuse following interaction
- **Web Proxy Logs:** Detect access to malicious URLs

---

## 6. Response Guidance
When spearphishing via service is suspected:
- Disable or suspend malicious service accounts
- Remove malicious messages or shared content
- Reset affected credentials and revoke sessions
- Review audit logs for scope and lateral messaging

Preserve service audit logs and message artifacts for investigation.

## 8. SOC Relevance
Service-based phishing is a growing blind spot for many SOCs, especially where monitoring focuses primarily on email. Visibility into SaaS audit logs and identity analytics is essential to detect and respond effectively.

---

## 9. Threat Actor Usage
This subtechnique is commonly used by:
- Ransomware affiliates and initial access brokers
- Financially motivated fraud actors
- Advanced persistent threat groups abusing cloud ecosystems

Confidence in widespread usage is high.

---

## 10. Campaign Usage
Spearphishing via service appears in:
- Cloud account takeover campaigns
- Internal phishing using compromised SaaS accounts
- Credential harvesting operations targeting enterprise collaboration tools

---

## 11. Malware Usage
Malware delivered or staged via service-based phishing commonly includes:
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S0650 - QakBot|QakBot]]
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S0367 - Emotet|Emotet]]
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S0266 - TrickBot|TrickBot]]

---

## 12. Mitigations
Effective mitigations include:
- Enforcing MFA across all SaaS platforms
- Monitoring and alerting on anomalous service messaging
- Restricting external messaging where possible
- User awareness training covering collaboration platform abuse
- Conditional access and risk-based authentication

---

## 13. Testing & Validation
Validation approaches include:
- Simulated phishing via collaboration platforms
- Purple team exercises targeting SaaS abuse scenarios
- Review of service audit log coverage and alerting

Successful validation results in early detection and rapid containment.
