## 1. Summary
**Silver Ticket (T1558.002)** is the forging of Kerberos **service tickets (TGS)** using a target service account’s password hash. Silver Tickets are typically **resource-scoped** (tied to a specific service), and can be harder to detect because they may be forged **without KDC interaction**.

## 2. Technical Overview
Defender-relevant behaviors:
- Forged **TGS/service tickets** that grant access to a specific service (e.g., database, web app, file service).
- **Reduced DC/KDC visibility** compared to Golden Tickets because KDC interaction may be minimized/absent depending on scenario.
- Preconditions often include access to a **service account hash**, commonly obtained via credential dumping or related ticket extraction techniques.

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
- Silver Tickets are narrower than Golden Tickets, but still high impact when:
  - The target service is privileged (admin tooling, management services, high-value databases)
  - The service account has broad rights or is reused across systems
- Detection strategy should emphasize:
  - **Service-side authentication/logon evidence**
  - Host/service baselines for where service accounts are used

## 4. Procedure Examples
MITRE ATT&CK procedure examples include:
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S0677 - AADInternals]] forging tickets using the password hash of the **AZUREADSSOACC** account.
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S0363 - Empire]] leveraging [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S0002 - Mimikatz]] to obtain/use Silver Tickets.
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S0002 - Mimikatz]] creating Silver Tickets via its Kerberos module.
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S1071 - Rubeus]] creating Silver Tickets.

## 5. Detection Guidance
Prioritize **service ticket anomalies** (aligned to ATT&CK Detection Strategy **DET0241**):

High-signal detection themes:
- **Malformed or unusual fields** in authentication/logon telemetry tied to service ticket use.
- **TGS/service ticket activity without expected KDC interaction** patterns.
- **Service account usage outside expected hosts/resources** (baseline where the service account should authenticate).
- Correlate with **credential access signals**, including suspicious processes accessing credential material.

Practical correlation ideas:
- Unusual service access attempt → check for prior normal authentication sequence → identify originating host and ticket properties.
- Service account suddenly used from a non-standard workstation or admin endpoint.

### Data Source Notes
Recommended telemetry:
- Windows security logs (logon/service access events on target systems)
- Kerberos-related events and authentication telemetry centralized in SIEM
- Endpoint telemetry for credential access behaviors on suspected operator hosts

## 6. Response Guidance
- **Contain**: isolate suspected operator endpoints; stop active credential access activity.
- **Service account response**: rotate impacted service account credentials; consider moving to managed service accounts when feasible.
- **Scope**: identify all services/resources accessed using the service account; hunt for parallel access using the same hash.
- **Hardening**: constrain service account privileges and limit where service accounts can log on.

## 8. SOC Relevance
- High relevance in AD environments with many service accounts.
- Best pivots:
  - Target system logon/service access anomalies → map to the service account → find where else it authenticated
  - Look for preceding credential access / dumping indicators

## 12. Mitigations
- **M1041 - Encrypt Sensitive Information**: enable AES Kerberos encryption (or another stronger algorithm) rather than RC4 where possible.
- **M1027 - Password Policies**: strong, long service account passwords (ideally 25+ characters) with periodic expiration; consider managed service accounts or vaulting.
- **M1026 - Privileged Account Management**: limit service accounts to minimal required privileges; avoid membership in privileged groups.

## 13. Testing & Validation
- Validate detection coverage for:
  - Service account authentication baseline violations (host/resource constraints)
  - Unusual service-ticket activity and malformed-field indicators
- Purple-team:
  - Run safe simulations that exercise service account logon anomalies (test accounts only) and validate alert routing + triage playbooks.
