## 1. Summary
Adversaries may mine CRM platforms (e.g., Salesforce, Dynamics 365, Zendesk) for customer data such as PII, purchase history, and support interactions. This data can enable downstream targeting (e.g., tailored phishing) and extortion. Suspicious access is often characterized by unusual high-volume record access or export events after anomalous sign-in patterns.

## 2. Technical Overview
Common CRM mining patterns include:
- High-volume viewing of customer records or PII-heavy objects.
- Bulk export behavior (report exports, data export features, API-based extraction).
- Automated API access (REST/API queries) using tokens or connected apps.
- Access by non-CRM roles or “rare” identities that do not typically perform CRM operations.

Defender-relevant behaviors:
- Access to large numbers of customer objects in short windows.
- Data export/report export events outside normal business hours or from new devices/geos.
- New OAuth/connected app authorizations associated with immediate data access.

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
- CRM usage varies widely by organization; effective detection requires baselining by role (sales vs support vs IT).
- SaaS CRM telemetry is often split across:
  - CRM platform event logs (API calls, exports, report downloads)
  - Identity provider logs (sign-ins, token issuance)
- CRM data frequently contains “secondary victim” information (customers), making impact assessment and regulatory response more complex.

## 4. Procedure Examples
- During **C0059 - Salesforce Data Exfiltration**, threat actors accessed and exfiltrated sensitive information from compromised Salesforce instances.

## 5. Detection Guidance
- **Export-centric alerts**: Alert on report exports/data exports that exceed baseline frequency/volume per user.
- **Object sensitivity**: Apply stricter monitoring for PII-heavy objects (contacts, cases, support notes, authentication-related fields).
- **Role mismatch**: Detect non-sales/non-support identities accessing large volumes of customer records.
- **Rare sign-in correlation**: Join abnormal CRM access to unusual sign-ins (rare geo/device, risky auth, new client/app).
- **Connected app scrutiny**: Monitor connected app authorizations and token usage patterns that enable sustained API extraction.

### Data Source Notes
- CRM event logs should include API access types (e.g., RestAPI), export/report events, and login events.
- Identity logs should capture sign-in anomalies and session creation context.
- MITRE detection strategy DET0550 highlights high-volume access and export behaviors, and provides example channels/events (DataExport, RestAPI, Login, ReportExport).

## 6. Response Guidance
1. **Contain identity and apps**: Revoke sessions/tokens; disable suspicious connected apps; rotate secrets.
2. **Scope accessed objects**: Determine which customer records/fields were accessed or exported and the timeframe.
3. **Assess customer impact**: Identify impacted customer segments and whether support interactions/PII were exposed.
4. **Hunt follow-on**: Watch for targeted phishing or fraud using CRM-derived details; coordinate with fraud/CS teams.
5. **Improve controls**: Enforce least-privilege CRM roles, tighten export permissions, and require stronger auth for exports.

## 8. SOC Relevance
High SOC signal:
- High-volume CRM access by atypical roles or newly observed devices/locations.
- Sudden spikes in exports/report exports.
- First-time connected app authorization followed by immediate bulk extraction.

Operational triage pivots:
- Which objects/fields were accessed, and export vs view-only.
- User history: typical login patterns, role, and usual export cadence.

## 12. Mitigations
- **Audit (M1047)**: Periodically review accounts/privileges for critical CRM data and export permissions.
- **Software Configuration (M1054)**: Implement data retention policies to archive/delete data no longer required.
- **User Account Management (M1018)**: Enforce least-privilege and strong access controls for CRM objects and exports.
- **User Training (M1017)**: Define acceptable handling/storage of customer data; minimize stored customer information to business needs.

## 13. Testing & Validation
- In a test CRM environment:
  - Generate controlled “export spikes” and confirm detection triggers.
  - Validate role-mismatch analytics (non-CRM test role accessing many records).
  - Validate sign-in anomaly correlation (test from new IP/device) without production impact.
- Ensure dashboards include:
  - Top exporters by volume
  - First-time exporter events
  - Connected app/token first-use
