# T1110.004 - Credential Stuffing

## 1. Summary
Credential Stuffing (T1110.004) is the use of breached username/password pairs from unrelated compromises to attempt access to target accounts, exploiting password reuse across services. It commonly produces many failures and may trigger lockouts, but adversaries often mitigate this via throttling and proxy infrastructure.

## 2. Technical Overview
Key characteristics:
- Inputs are **credential pairs** (username + password) sourced from breach dumps.
- Attempts are made against exposed authentication portals (IdP/SSO, email, VPN, remote services).
- Distinctive telemetry shape: many different usernames with different passwords from a source (or distributed sources), often with bot-like cadence or “humanized” throttling.

Important defender context:
- Successful access strongly implies **password reuse** and may indicate broader account compromise risk (personal ↔ corporate overlap).

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
- Parent technique: [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/TA0006 - Credential Access/T1110 - Brute Force|T1110]]
- Distinguish from:
  - **Password Spraying (T1110.003)**: one/few passwords across many accounts.
  - **Password Guessing (T1110.001)**: common/dictionary guesses without breached pairs.
- Credential stuffing often overlaps with identity-protection controls (breached password detection, risk-based auth).

## 5. Detection Guidance
Detection objective: **identify reuse of breached credential pairs and the characteristic “many users / many passwords” attack shape**.

Recommended detections:
- **High-cardinality username failures**
  - Many distinct usernames failing from one IP/client fingerprint within a short window.
- **Credential-pair rotation**
  - Rapid failures with varying usernames and varying passwords, often with consistent UA/client hints.
- **Cloud/IdP targeting**
  - Many sign-in failures across different users from the same source IP against IdPs (Azure AD/Okta/etc.), including repeated attempts across multiple identities.
- **Bot/proxy indicators**
  - IP reputation, residential proxy ASNs, TOR exit nodes, abnormal TLS/client fingerprints.
- **Success pivot detection**
  - First successful auth after a wave of high-cardinality failures, especially from a new device/geo.

### Data Source Notes
This technique page does not present an explicit ATT&CK “Data Sources” list in the technique header; prioritize:
- IdP sign-in logs with failure reasons and risk/CA outcomes
- Edge/WAF/reverse proxy auth endpoint telemetry
- Application authentication audit logs
- Network device AAA/syslog (if targeting management plane)
- Account security tooling outputs (breached password checks, risk scoring)

## 6. Response Guidance
- Immediately block/limit abusive sources and enable bot mitigation on auth endpoints.
- Force password resets for accounts with successful suspicious logons; revoke sessions/tokens.
- Turn on breached-password protection (where supported) and prevent password reuse.
- Increase authentication assurance for affected apps (MFA, phishing-resistant methods, device posture checks).
- Conduct scope analysis: identify other services where the same principals may be at risk.

## 12. Mitigations
ATT&CK-listed mitigations for this subtechnique include:
- **M1036 - Account Use Policies**: lockouts/conditional access and restrictions on risky sources.
- **M1032 - Multi-factor Authentication**: reduces the value of reused passwords.
- **M1027 - Password Policies**: prevent password reuse and weak passwords; apply breached-password controls where possible.
- **M1018 - User Account Management**: proactive resets for accounts known to be in breached datasets or implicated in stuffing attempts.

## 13. Testing & Validation
- Validate detection for high-cardinality failures and credential-pair rotation patterns.
- Confirm IdP telemetry includes sufficient failure reasons and risk signals.
- Exercise response automation in a controlled environment to avoid unintended lockout of legitimate users.
