# Indicator Removal on Host: Network Share Connection Removal (T1070.005)

## 1. Summary
Network Share Connection Removal describes adversaries **removing evidence of connections to network file shares** used during lateral movement, staging, or data access. By clearing or disconnecting share mappings and cached connection artifacts, attackers reduce visibility into their movement across the environment.

Attackers use this technique to:
- Conceal lateral movement paths
- Remove forensic artifacts related to SMB/NFS access
- Reduce attribution of accessed systems
- Obscure data staging or exfiltration preparation

---

## 2. Technical Overview
Operating systems maintain artifacts related to network share access, including active connections, cached credentials, and session metadata. Adversaries remove these artifacts by:

- Disconnecting mapped network drives
- Clearing cached SMB/NFS connection data
- Removing share session records
- Deleting configuration artifacts related to share usage

Typical environments:
- Windows environments using SMB shares
- Linux/macOS systems accessing NFS or SMB mounts
- Enterprise networks with shared file servers

Indicators include:
- Abrupt termination of share connections
- Missing artifacts where access is otherwise implied
- Authentication events without persistent connection records
- Lateral movement activity without corresponding share traces

---

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
Key considerations for T1070.005:
- Commonly follows **Remote Services (T1021)** usage
- Often paired with **Network Share Discovery (T1135)**
- Used as a cleanup step after lateral movement or staging
- Reduces fidelity of network-based investigations

---

## 4. Procedure Examples
Representative adversary behaviors include:
- Disconnecting mapped drives after use
- Removing share access artifacts post-lateral movement
- Clearing cached share authentication data
- Cleaning up temporary mounts used for staging

*(Examples are representative and intentionally non-operational.)*

---

## 5. Detection Guidance
Detection should focus on **share access lifecycle anomalies**:
- Monitor share connection creation followed by rapid removal
- Correlate authentication to file servers with missing share artifacts
- Detect share access without expected persistence
- Correlate share removal with suspicious process execution

### Data Source Notes
- **SMB/NFS logs**: Primary visibility into share access
- **Authentication logs**: Attribute share usage to identities
- **EDR telemetry**: Correlate share usage with processes

Common false positives:
- Legitimate temporary share access
- User-initiated drive disconnections
- Automated cleanup scripts

Tuning guidance:
- Baseline normal share usage duration
- Elevate alerts when share removal coincides with other intrusion indicators

---

## 6. Response Guidance
When suspected:
1. Identify recently accessed network shares
2. Correlate share access with authentication and process telemetry
3. Investigate accessed systems for follow-on activity
4. Expand hunt for similar behavior across hosts

## 8. SOC Relevance
T1070.005 is important when:
- Lateral movement is suspected
- File server access is implied but poorly evidenced
- Investigations show gaps in share usage timelines

It often explains **“how did they move files without evidence?”**

## 10. Campaign Usage
Observed in:
- Ransomware staging phases

Associated with:

---

## 12. Mitigations
Recommended mitigations:
- Centralize and retain SMB/NFS logs
- Monitor share access duration and frequency
- Restrict unnecessary share access
- Correlate share access with endpoint telemetry

---

## 13. Testing & Validation
Validation approaches:
- Create and remove network share connections in lab environments
- Validate alerts on short-lived share access
- Test SOC correlation between authentication and share logs
