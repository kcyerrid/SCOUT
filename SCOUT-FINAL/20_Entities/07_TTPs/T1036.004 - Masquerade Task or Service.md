# Masquerading: Masquerade Task or Service (T1036.004)

## 1. Summary
Masquerade Task or Service describes adversaries **creating or modifying scheduled tasks, services, or background jobs with names and configurations that resemble legitimate system components**. This deception helps malicious persistence blend into normal system operations and evade detection.

Attackers use this technique to:
- Hide persistence mechanisms in plain sight
- Evade analyst scrutiny during task/service reviews
- Reduce the likelihood of removal
- Maintain long-term access under benign-looking artifacts

---

## 2. Technical Overview
Operating systems rely on scheduled tasks, services, and background jobs for legitimate automation and functionality. Adversaries exploit this by:

- Creating malicious tasks with names similar to system or vendor tasks
- Registering services that mimic legitimate service naming conventions
- Modifying existing tasks or services to execute malicious payloads
- Using descriptions, paths, and startup settings that appear normal

Common targets include:
- Windows Scheduled Tasks and Services
- Linux cron jobs and systemd services
- macOS launch agents and daemons
- Cloud scheduled jobs and automation services

Indicators include:
- Tasks or services with benign names executing suspicious binaries
- Startup artifacts pointing to unusual file paths
- Descriptions that do not match actual behavior
- Persistence artifacts lacking associated documentation or change tickets

---

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
Key considerations for T1036.004:
- Strong overlap with **Scheduled Task/Job (T1053)** and **Boot or Logon Autostart Execution (T1547)**
- Often combined with **Hide Artifacts (T1564)**
- Particularly effective against manual review processes
- Can persist across reboots and user sessions

This technique emphasizes **deception of defenders**, not technical stealth.

---

## 4. Procedure Examples
Representative adversary behaviors include:
- Creating scheduled tasks with names similar to OS maintenance jobs
- Registering services with vendor-like names and descriptions
- Modifying legitimate tasks to execute additional payloads
- Deploying persistence that appears indistinguishable from system activity

*(Examples are representative and intentionally non-operational.)*

---

## 5. Detection Guidance
Detection should focus on **semantic and behavioral mismatches**:
- Compare task/service names against known-good baselines
- Alert on startup artifacts executing from non-standard paths
- Detect persistence artifacts created shortly after compromise
- Correlate persistence creation with suspicious execution or privilege escalation

### Data Source Notes
- **Scheduled task logs**: Detect creation and modification
- **Service configuration logs**: Attribute changes and execution paths
- **Registry and startup logs**: Identify persistence locations
- **EDR telemetry**: Provide execution context and lineage

Common false positives:
- Legitimate software installers
- Enterprise management tooling
- System updates and patches

Tuning guidance:
- Maintain allowlists of approved tasks and services
- Increase severity when masquerading artifacts appear alongside other intrusion indicators

---

## 6. Response Guidance
When suspected:
1. Validate the legitimacy of the task or service
2. Inspect execution paths and associated binaries
3. Correlate creation time with intrusion timeline
4. Disable and remove malicious persistence safely
5. Hunt for similarly named artifacts across hosts

## 8. SOC Relevance
T1036.004 explains persistence that:
- Appears legitimate during cursory review
- Survives long periods undetected
- Is missed by name-based hunting

SOC teams must scrutinize **what tasks do**, not just what they are called.

---

## 9. Threat Actor Usage
Commonly used by:
- Advanced persistent threats
- Ransomware affiliates
- Intruders seeking durable, low-noise persistence

---

## 10. Campaign Usage
Observed in:
- Long-dwell espionage operations
- Ransomware staging phases
- Intrusions emphasizing stealth and resilience

---

## 11. Malware Usage
Associated with:
- Backdoors and implants
- Post-exploitation frameworks
- Malware requiring reboot persistence

---

## 12. Mitigations
Recommended mitigations:
- Baseline scheduled tasks and services
- Require approvals for persistence changes
- Monitor startup execution paths
- Apply least-privilege controls

---

## 13. Testing & Validation
Validation approaches:
- Create benign scheduled tasks with misleading names in lab environments
- Validate alerts on suspicious persistence creation
- Test SOC workflows for masqueraded services
- Ensure detections consider behavior and execution paths
