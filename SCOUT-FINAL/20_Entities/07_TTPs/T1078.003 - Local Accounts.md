# Local Accounts (T1078.003)

## 1. Summary
Local Accounts describes adversary abuse of credentials tied to accounts that exist only on individual hosts rather than being centrally managed by a directory service. This subtechnique is commonly leveraged to establish persistence, escalate privileges, or facilitate lateral movement in environments where local administrator credentials are reused across systems.

Because activity leverages legitimate authentication mechanisms, malicious use may blend with routine administrative behavior at the host level.

---

## 2. Technical Overview
Adversaries obtain local account credentials through credential dumping, password reuse, offline hash cracking, or direct knowledge of configured passwords. Once obtained, credentials are used to authenticate locally or remotely to hosts where the account exists.

Common technical mechanisms include:
- Local interactive logon
- Remote authentication via SMB, RDP, or SSH
- Use of local administrator accounts for privileged access

Artifacts include successful authentication events, process execution under local accounts, and access to administrative resources.

---

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
T1078.003 differs from other Valid Account subtechniques in that:
- Account scope is limited to individual hosts
- Centralized identity controls may not apply
- Risk increases significantly with credential reuse

This subtechnique is especially relevant in environments lacking local admin password management solutions.

---

## 4. Procedure Examples
Observed adversary procedures include:
- Authenticating to multiple hosts using the same local administrator credentials
- Establishing persistence through local admin access
- Leveraging local accounts to stage tools or execute payloads

Analysts may observe repeated successful local logons across multiple systems by the same account.

---

## 5. Detection Guidance
Detection should emphasize:
- Local administrator logons outside expected maintenance windows
- Authentication events involving shared local accounts
- Lateral movement patterns using local credentials
- Privileged process execution shortly after authentication

Endpoint telemetry and authentication logs are primary detection sources.

### Data Source Notes
- **Authentication Logs:** Essential for identifying account usage patterns
- **Process Execution:** High signal when correlated with logon events

---

## 6. Response Guidance
When Local Account abuse is suspected:
- Validate whether activity is authorized
- Reset or rotate local account credentials
- Identify scope of credential reuse
- Investigate for follow-on persistence or lateral movement

Preserve endpoint and authentication logs for forensic analysis.

## 8. SOC Relevance
Local Account abuse remains a frequent enabler of ransomware deployment and hands-on-keyboard intrusions. Organizations without strong local credential hygiene face elevated risk and reduced detection fidelity.

---

## 9. Threat Actor Usage
This subtechnique is widely used by:
- Ransomware affiliates conducting lateral movement
- Post-exploitation operators validating privilege boundaries
- Initial access brokers assessing host value

Confidence in widespread usage is high.

---

## 10. Campaign Usage
Local Accounts appear in:
- Ransomware intrusions prior to encryption
- Post-compromise staging and tooling deployment
- Lateral movement preparation phases

---

## 12. Mitigations
Effective mitigations include:
- Implementing Local Administrator Password Solution (LAPS)
- Enforcing unique local admin credentials
- Restricting remote logon using local accounts
- Monitoring privileged local authentication events

---

## 13. Testing & Validation
Validation approaches include:
- Atomic Red Team tests for local account abuse
- Purple team simulations of lateral movement using local credentials
- Manual review of local admin logon behavior

Successful validation results in timely alerts with low false positives.
