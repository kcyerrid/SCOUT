## 1. Summary
**AS-REP Roasting (T1558.004)** targets domain accounts with **Kerberos preauthentication disabled**. Adversaries can request AS-REP responses that contain data protected by the user’s password, enabling **offline password cracking**.

## 2. Technical Overview
Defender-relevant behaviors:
- **Enumeration** of accounts with preauthentication disabled (directory queries or iterative requests).
- **AS-REQ without preauth** followed by an **AS-REP** that includes crackable material for those accounts.
- **Offline cracking** typically occurs off-host; on-host evidence is primarily in authentication and directory-query telemetry.
- Follow-on behavior includes use of recovered credentials for access and lateral movement.

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
- The largest risk driver is **configuration drift** (accounts set to “Do not require Kerberos preauthentication”).
- Detection strategy often needs:
  - Baseline of legitimate accounts that must remain without preauth (if any)
  - Tight monitoring of **changes** to the preauth setting
- This technique is commonly paired with other credential access and account discovery behaviors.

## 4. Procedure Examples
MITRE ATT&CK procedure examples include:
- [[30_CIPHER/05_Malware/S1071 - Rubeus]] revealing credentials of accounts with Kerberos preauthentication disabled via AS-REP roasting.

## 5. Detection Guidance
Build detections around **account configuration + AS-REQ/AS-REP patterns**:

High-signal detection themes (aligned to ATT&CK Detection Strategy **DET0113**):
- **AS-REQ/AS-REP volume anomalies** per requesting account/host, especially iterative patterns across many usernames.
- **Requests for preauth-disabled users** outside baseline.
- **RC4/weak encryption usage** in AS-REP responses where stronger algorithms are expected.
- **Change monitoring**:
  - Alerts on accounts newly configured without Kerberos preauthentication (especially privileged accounts).

Correlations to raise fidelity:
- Account discovery → AS-REQ bursts → subsequent successful logons by newly targeted accounts.

### Data Source Notes
Recommended telemetry:
- Domain Controller Kerberos logs (AS-REQ/AS-REP related events)
- Directory/audit logs for account attribute changes (preauth setting)
- Identity analytics for post-roast credential usage (new hosts/devices/geos)

## 6. Response Guidance
- **Contain**: isolate suspected requester hosts if active roasting attempts are confirmed.
- **Remediate configuration**: enable preauthentication for accounts whenever possible; validate that exceptions are documented and justified.
- **Credential actions**: rotate passwords for affected accounts; prioritize privileged accounts.
- **Scope**: identify which accounts were targeted and whether any subsequent successful authentication occurred.

## 8. SOC Relevance
- High relevance because it is often detectable via DC logs and configuration monitoring.
- Best pivots:
  - Identify requesting host/account patterns (bursts, breadth of usernames)
  - Validate whether any accounts recently had preauth disabled
  - Hunt for successful logons by targeted accounts after the roasting window

## 12. Mitigations
- **M1047 - Audit**: ensure Kerberos preauthentication is enabled whenever possible; audit and monitor changes to the setting.
- **M1041 - Encrypt Sensitive Information**: enable AES Kerberos encryption (or another stronger algorithm) rather than RC4 where possible.
- **M1027 - Password Policies**: strong password length and complexity (especially for accounts with elevated privileges).

## 13. Testing & Validation
- Validate detection coverage for:
  - AS-REQ/AS-REP request anomalies and iterative request patterns
  - Alerts on changes to “Do not require Kerberos preauthentication”
  - Follow-on logons by targeted accounts after suspicious request windows
- Periodic control review:
  - Inventory and justify any accounts that have preauthentication disabled.
