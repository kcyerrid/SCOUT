# T1556.009 – Conditional Access Policies

## 1. Technique Overview
**Conditional Access Policies (T1556.009)** is a sub-technique of [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1556 - Modify Authentication Process|T1556]] in **MITRE ATT&CK v18**. Adversaries abuse **cloud conditional access controls**—such as those in Microsoft Entra ID (Azure AD) or other cloud identity providers—to **bypass MFA**, reduce authentication requirements, or selectively exempt accounts, devices, or locations from security enforcement.

Because conditional access operates at the **identity control plane**, abuse can provide durable access without deploying malware or touching endpoints.

## 2. Adversary Objectives
Adversaries use conditional access abuse to:
- Bypass MFA or step-up authentication requirements
- Maintain access after credential rotation or incident response
- Persist via policy-based exceptions rather than accounts
- Reduce visibility by aligning access with “approved” conditions

## 3. Common Abuse Patterns
- Creating conditional access exclusions for attacker-controlled accounts
- Modifying policies to allow legacy or password-only authentication
- Disabling device compliance or location-based restrictions
- Downgrading risk-based sign-in enforcement
- Leveraging global admin access to subtly weaken policies

## 4. Detection Considerations
Detection requires **cloud identity and policy telemetry**, including:
- Monitoring creation, modification, or deletion of conditional access policies
- Auditing exclusions added to existing policies
- Correlating successful sign-ins with reduced authentication challenges
- Reviewing administrative activity affecting identity security posture
- Comparing policy state against known-good baselines

## 5. Defensive Mitigations
- Enforce approval workflows and logging for conditional access changes
- Require MFA for all identity and access administrators
- Monitor and alert on policy modifications and exclusions
- Regularly audit conditional access configurations
- Limit standing global administrator privileges

## 6. Operational Impact
If successful, T1556.009 can:
- Neutralize MFA and conditional enforcement enterprise-wide
- Enable long-term persistence without endpoint artifacts
- Facilitate privilege escalation and lateral movement in cloud resources
- Complicate incident response due to policy-level abuse

## 7. Analyst Notes
Conditional access abuse is often **low-noise and high-impact**. Analysts should treat unexpected authentication success—especially without MFA—as a potential **policy compromise**, not merely credential theft. Incident response must include **policy review and restoration**, not just account remediation.
