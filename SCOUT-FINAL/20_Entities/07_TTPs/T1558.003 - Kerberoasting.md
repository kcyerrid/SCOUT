## 1. Summary
**Kerberoasting (T1558.003)** is the collection of Kerberos **service tickets (TGS)** for SPN-associated service accounts and subsequent offline password cracking attempts against the encrypted portions of those tickets. It is a common and impactful path to service account compromise in Active Directory environments.

## 2. Technical Overview
Defender-relevant behaviors:
- **Requesting TGS tickets for SPNs** from a domain controller (DC), often in unusual volume or targeting unusual services.
- **Encryption-type signals**: RC4 (etype 23) is commonly associated with crackable material in many environments; AES-only environments change the signal profile.
- **Offline cracking** typically happens off-host; on-host evidence may be limited to ticket requests and tool/process activity.
- **Follow-on** after password recovery: service account authentication, lateral movement, and privilege escalation.

## 3. Subtechnique Considerations
- Kerberoasting is often performed from a low-privileged foothold because any authenticated domain account can request many service tickets.
- Detection engineering should emphasize:
  - **TGS request baselining** (who requests what service tickets, how often, and from where)
  - **Service account usage baselines** and subsequent authentication changes after suspected roasting
- Environment hardening (strong service account passwords, managed service accounts, AES enforcement) materially reduces risk.

## 5. Detection Guidance
Prioritize DC-side Kerberos telemetry and request-pattern analytics (aligned to ATT&CK Detection Strategy **DET0157**):

High-signal detection themes:
- **Anomalous TGS requests** (e.g., Windows Event ID 4769) including:
  - Unusual volume of service ticket requests over short intervals
  - Requests targeting rare/high-value services outside baseline
  - Accounts requesting tickets they do not normally request
- **Encryption-type deviations** (including unexpected RC4 usage where AES is expected)
- **Process correlation**:
  - Suspicious tooling/process activity on endpoints correlated to Kerberos request bursts
- **Post-roast behavior**:
  - New or changed authentication activity by service accounts after suspected roasting window

Triage enrichment:
- Identify the requesting host, user, SPN, and target service.
- Compare against baseline for that requester and for the service account.

### Data Source Notes
Recommended telemetry:
- Domain Controller Kerberos logs (TGS request events)
- Identity analytics (service account sign-ins, unusual hosts, new geos/devices)
- Endpoint telemetry (PowerShell, process creation, unusual module loads) for requesting hosts

## 6. Response Guidance
- **Contain**: isolate or restrict the requesting host/account if active Kerberoasting is confirmed.
- **Service account actions**: rotate impacted service account credentials; consider migration to managed service accounts where feasible.
- **Scope**: determine which SPNs were targeted; hunt for subsequent successful logons by those accounts from unusual hosts.
- **Hardening**: enforce strong service account password policies and reduce unnecessary SPN exposure.

## 8. SOC Relevance
- High-frequency technique with strong detection potential if DC logs are centralized and baselined.
- Best pivots:
  - Identify high-volume/rare TGS requesters → map to SPNs → track service account auth after the request window
  - Correlate with suspicious PowerShell/tooling on the requesting endpoint

## 12. Mitigations
- **M1041 - Encrypt Sensitive Information**: enable AES Kerberos encryption (or another stronger algorithm) rather than RC4 where possible.
- **M1027 - Password Policies**: strong, long service account passwords with periodic rotation; consider managed service accounts or vaulting.
- **M1026 - Privileged Account Management**: limit service accounts to minimal required privileges; avoid privileged group membership.

## 13. Testing & Validation
- Validate detections for:
  - Abnormal TGS request volumes and rare-SPN targeting
  - Encryption-type anomalies relative to policy/baseline
  - Correlation of suspicious endpoint activity with Kerberos request bursts
- Purple-team:
  - Use test service accounts/SPNs and validate alerts (no real cracking) and SOC triage workflows.
