# T1137.006 – Add-ins

## 1. Technique Overview
**Add-ins (T1137.006)** is a sub-technique of [[20_Entities/07_TTPs/T1137 - Office Application Startup|T1137]] in **MITRE ATT&CK v18**. Adversaries abuse **Microsoft Office add-in mechanisms** to achieve persistence by ensuring malicious add-ins are loaded automatically whenever an Office application (such as Word, Excel, PowerPoint, or Outlook) is launched.

Office add-ins may include **COM add-ins, VSTO add-ins, or other supported extension models**, and are commonly trusted in enterprise environments, making them a reliable persistence vector.

## 2. Adversary Objectives
Adversaries use Office add-in persistence to:
- Achieve repeated execution tied to normal Office usage
- Maintain persistence without classic autorun registry keys or startup folders
- Blend malicious behavior into legitimate Office extensibility features
- Reduce user suspicion by masquerading as productivity extensions

## 3. Common Abuse Patterns
- Installing malicious COM or VSTO add-ins on compromised systems
- Registering add-ins via Office configuration or the Windows Registry
- Masquerading add-ins as legitimate business or productivity tools
- Leveraging user-level add-in registration to avoid administrative privileges
- Using add-ins to load secondary payloads or establish command-and-control

## 4. Detection Considerations
Detection relies on **Office configuration, registry, and execution telemetry**, including:
- Auditing installed Office add-ins across endpoints
- Monitoring registry keys and configuration locations used to register add-ins
- Detecting Office applications loading unexpected DLLs or assemblies
- Correlating Office startup with anomalous child process or network activity
- Baseline comparison of known-good add-ins within the environment

## 5. Defensive Mitigations
- Restrict installation of Office add-ins through administrative policy
- Monitor and alert on new or modified add-in registrations
- Enforce trusted publisher and code signing requirements
- Regularly inventory and review Office add-ins enterprise-wide
- Remove unauthorized add-ins during incident response

## 6. Operational Impact
If successful, T1137.006 can:
- Provide reliable user-context persistence
- Enable execution without macro enablement
- Persist across reboots and application restarts
- Complicate detection due to trusted application execution context

## 7. Analyst Notes
Office add-ins are frequently overlooked during incident response compared to macros and templates. Analysts should ensure **add-in inventories and baselines** are included in persistence-hunting workflows, especially following phishing or document-based initial access events.
